# Support continuity review

Reviewed 2026-10-09 against engine model 13 (`8d5319d`), following the first five
quiz-balancing passes. This is a dated research and design record, not live policy
or an implemented support model. These are
policy and workflow observations, not scores or predictions of project survival.
Edition scope follows the current repository assessment. Upstream statements
establish a documented mechanism; they do not independently establish its success
rate, response time, or future funding.

Evidence labels: **policy** means a retrieved explicit support window or package
scope; **workflow** means retrieved update, maintenance, security, or release
process documentation; **partial** means relevant documentation was retrieved but
a whole-edition support commitment remains unresolved. These labels describe this
review's evidence, not project quality. Retrieval failures are not negative evidence.

## Decision and intent

The review does not justify adding a numeric support or project-longevity prior.
Keep the matching model unchanged on this dimension. Project age, size, base
ancestry and our ability to retrieve a web page must not become automatic quality
bonuses or penalties. A smaller project can document a careful release process;
a long-lived upstream does not guarantee maintenance of derivative integrations.

If support information is added to the structured assessment later, keep separate
fields for edition/channel, release stage, published support window and package
scope, documented update/upgrade/recovery paths, evidence date/sources and unresolved
claims. These are proposed categories, not current schema fields. Assessment
confidence describes our evidence; it must not silently reduce a distro's fit.

Current freshness, upkeep and identity answers do not cleanly express either
wanting to remain on one supported release or prioritizing project continuity.
Do not infer that preference or add a question solely to obtain a desired ranking.
Revisit a support preference only when a concrete user choice warrants it.

### Tumbleweed and Rhino reachability

In the reviewed dataset, Tumbleweed is at least as capable as Rhino on all ten
axes, with equal freshness. Under the current one-sided capability shortfalls,
Tumbleweed therefore never has a lower capability score. Their scored trait
and specialist differences favor Tumbleweed or tie. Rhino can receive +2 for its
fully satisfied soft-preference group; Tumbleweed's breadth advantage is 0.6.
Thus `Rhino raw − Tumbleweed raw ≤ 2 − 0.6 = 1.4`.

More than 1.4 unconditional additional raw points for Tumbleweed would eliminate
Rhino's winning route. A diagnostic +0.5 retained all 29 strict winning witnesses
but changed 2,194 of 100,000 sampled winners, mostly taking wins from other
established projects. A flat bonus is neither a narrowly targeted correction nor
a source-backed support model. These conclusions depend on the reviewed profiles
and scoring rules; reassess the bound if either changes.

The existing capabilities already favor Tumbleweed on several relevant dimensions.
Do not force equal exposure, but preserve a defensible first-place route for every
profile in the curated roster. Adding a distinct Rhino workflow preference would
require evidence of that preference and user intent, rather than compensatory points.
